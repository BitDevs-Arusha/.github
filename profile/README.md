## About

**BitDevs Arusha** is a technical Bitcoin community in Arusha, Tanzania, for people who want to discuss, research and contribute to the development of Bitcoin and related protocols and software. Our chapter began at **Bitcoin block 968,072**.

Our goal is to build a strong local community of people who understand Bitcoin at a technical level, can critically examine its development, and are able to contribute to its open-source ecosystem.

We follow the global BitDevs model. Each month focuses on one technical subject, built around real Bitcoin Improvement Proposals (BIPs), Bitcoin Core pull requests, research papers, software releases and open technical questions.

## Socratic Seminars

Our monthly meetups use the [Socratic Seminar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socratic_method#Socratic_seminar) format. A moderator guides the conversation instead of giving a lecture, and participants examine the technical material together through open questions, respectful disagreement and hands-on demonstrations or code review.

Discussion topics are prepared ahead of each meetup from sources such as:

- Pull requests in open-source projects (Bitcoin Core, LND, Core Lightning, BDK and more)
- BIPs, BOLTs and research papers
- [Bitcoin Optech](https://bitcoinops.org) newsletters, mailing lists and technical blog posts
- Network monitors and on-chain data

Discussions follow the **Chatham House Rule**: you may use what is shared, but not reveal who said it without permission. Sessions are not recorded by default.

## 12-Month Syllabus (2026–2027)

| Month | Topic |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Introduction to BitDevs and the Bitcoin development ecosystem |
| 2 | Bitcoin transactions, UTXOs and mempool policy |
| 3 | Bitcoin Script, Taproot and Schnorr signatures |
| 4 | Bitcoin privacy and transaction confidentiality |
| 5 | Lightning Network architecture and channel mechanics |
| 6 | Lightning improvements: BOLT12, splicing and channel factories |
| 7 | Bitcoin scalability and alternative transaction constructions |
| 8 | Bitcoin node architecture, networking and decentralisation |
| 9 | Bitcoin mining, proof of work and network security |
| 10 | Bitcoin security, cryptography and post-quantum research |
| 11 | Bitcoin development, code review and open-source contribution |
| 12 | Emerging Bitcoin research and the future of protocol development |

By the end of the programme, participants should be able to read and critically discuss BIPs, pull requests and research, understand Bitcoin's security, privacy, scalability and decentralisation trade-offs, and find opportunities to contribute to open-source Bitcoin projects.
